**🔧 REPLIT AGENT PROMPT --- EU AI Act Article Grounding (Deterministic)**
------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Objective**

Ground the existing deterministic compliance rules in **explicit EU AI Act articles**, so the system can prove *which legal provisions justify each risk outcome*.

Do **not** introduce free-form legal reasoning or LLM interpretation.

This is **traceability**, not legal analysis.

* * * * *

**📐 REQUIRED IMPLEMENTATION PLAN (FOLLOW EXACTLY)**
----------------------------------------------------

* * * * *

### **STEP 1 --- Create an EU AI Act Reference Corpus (STATIC, SAFE)**

Create a new file:

```
client/src/lib/euAiAct.ts
```

Define a **minimal, authoritative reference object** containing:

-   Article number

-   Official title

-   Short neutral description (summary, not interpretation)

Example:

```
export const EU_AI_ACT_ARTICLES = {
  "Article 6": {
    title: "Classification of high-risk AI systems",
    summary:
      "Defines criteria under which AI systems are classified as high-risk, including intended purpose and deployment context."
  },
  "Article 52": {
    title: "Transparency obligations for certain AI systems",
    summary:
      "Requires transparency obligations for AI systems that interact with humans or generate synthetic content."
  },
  "Article 28b": {
    title: "Obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models",
    summary:
      "Defines obligations applicable to providers of general-purpose and foundation AI models."
  }
}
```

⚠️ Do NOT paste full legal text.

⚠️ Do NOT summarize in a way that implies legal judgment.

* * * * *

### **STEP 2 --- Extend Rules to Include Explicit Article References**

Modify your existing rules file (e.g. rules.ts) so **each rule includes regulation grounding**.

Example rule shape:

```
{
  id: "EU-AIA-PUBLIC-DEPLOYMENT",
  description: "AI system deployed to the general public",
  risk: "HIGH",
  conditions: {
    user_type: "General Public"
  },
  regulation_refs: [
    {
      regulation: "EU AI Act",
      article: "Article 6"
    }
  ]
}
```

Rules MUST:

-   Reference one or more articles

-   Never contain legal reasoning

-   Represent **policy logic only**

* * * * *

### **STEP 3 --- Propagate Article References Through the Audit Result**

Update the output of auditModel() to include:

```
applied_articles: string[]
```

This list must be:

-   Derived from triggered_rules[].regulation_refs

-   Deduplicated

-   Ordered consistently

Do NOT infer articles dynamically.

* * * * *

### **STEP 4 --- Update the Audit Trail UI (Right Pane)**

In **Step 5: Regulatory Mapping Applied**, replace generic labels with:

-   Article badges (e.g. Article 6, Article 28b)

-   Tooltip or expandable panel showing:

    -   Article title

    -   Neutral summary

    -   "Rule(s) that triggered this article"

Example UI copy:

> "This risk classification is grounded in EU AI Act Article 6, based on the triggered deployment and model type rules."

* * * * *

### **STEP 5 --- Add a "Legal Basis" Inspector (Read-Only)**

Under each article badge, allow users to expand:

**Legal Basis**

-   Article title

-   Summary

-   Triggering rules (IDs + descriptions)

Explicitly label:

> "This system applies deterministic rules derived from the EU AI Act. It does not interpret or replace legal advice."

* * * * *

### **STEP 6 --- Preserve & Strengthen Disclaimers**

Add or preserve the following disclaimer at the bottom of the audit trail:

> "Regulatory grounding reflects explicit mappings between deterministic rules and EU AI Act articles. This system does not perform legal interpretation or provide legal advice."

Do NOT remove existing disclaimers.

* * * * *

**🚫 EXPLICIT NON-GOALS (DO NOT IMPLEMENT)**
--------------------------------------------

-   No LLM reasoning over regulation text

-   No full EU AI Act ingestion

-   No RAG

-   No "does this violate the law?" answers

-   No compliance guarantees

* * * * *

**✅ VERIFICATION CHECKLIST**
----------------------------

After implementation:

-   Every triggered rule maps to ≥1 EU AI Act article

-   Every displayed article is traceable to ≥1 triggered rule

-   Article summaries are neutral and factual

-   No UI text implies legal judgment

-   Changing inputs changes which articles appear

-   The system remains deterministic
